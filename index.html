Description

This simulation solves the mean-field Ising model exactly within the approximation. The derivations below go step by step from the Hamiltonian to everything plotted above. In the simulation, units are kBTc = qJ = 1.

1. Mean-field Hamiltonian

Start from the Ising Hamiltonian with N spins si = ±1, each with q nearest neighbors:

ℋ = −J Σ⟨ij⟩ sisj − h Σi si

Write each spin as its average plus a fluctuation, si = m + δsi, where m = ⟨si⟩. Then, exactly,

sisj = m² + m(si − m) + m(sj − m) + δsiδsj

Dropping δsiδsj, which means assuming ⟨sisj⟩ ≈ ⟨si⟩⟨sj⟩, leaves sisj ≈ −m² + m(si + sj). There are Nq/2 bonds, and each spin sits on q of them, so Σ⟨ij⟩(si + sj) = q Σi si. Therefore

ℋ ≈ NqJm²/2 − (qJm + h) Σi si

Each spin now feels an effective field heff = qJm + h, and no term couples two spins.

2. Partition function and g(h, T)

Because the spins are decoupled, the sum over all 2N configurations factorizes into N identical single-spin sums:

Z = e−βNqJm²/2 Πi Σs=±1 eβheffs = e−βNqJm²/2 [2 cosh β(qJm + h)]N

With h held fixed, −kBT ln Z plays the role of a Gibbs free energy. Per spin:

g(h, T) = qJm²/2 − kBT ln[2 cosh β(qJm + h)]     (Eq. 2)

This is the dashed curve in the first graph, plotted as a function of the trial value m.

3. Self-consistency equation

Differentiate Eq. 2 with respect to h. Using d(ln cosh x)/dx = tanh x,

m = −(∂g/∂h)T = kBT · β tanh β(qJm + h) = tanh β(qJm + h)     (Eq. 3)

Setting ∂g/∂m = qJm − qJ tanh β(qJm + h) = 0 gives the same equation, so the solutions are the extrema of g. The second graph finds them as the crossings of tanh β(qJm + h) with the line y = m.

4. Critical temperature

At h = 0, m = 0 always solves Eq. 3. For small m, tanh x ≈ x, so the right side starts out as βqJm, with slope βqJ. Because tanh saturates at ±1, the curve crosses y = m again at ±m0 only if it starts out steeper than the line:

βqJ > 1   ⟹   kBTc = qJ

5. Helmholtz route: a(m)

Treat each spin as independent, with p± = (1 ± m)/2, so that ⟨s⟩ = p+ − p− = m. The entropy per spin is that of one site, and the energy uses ⟨sisj⟩ ≈ m² on each of q/2 bonds per spin:

s = −kB[p+ ln p+ + p− ln p−],    e = −qJm²/2
a(m) = e − Ts = −qJm²/2 + kBT[p+ ln p+ + p− ln p−]

In a field, minimize a(m) − hm; this is the solid curve. The derivative of the entropy term is (kBT/2) ln[(1 + m)/(1 − m)] = kBT tanh−1m, so

∂(a − hm)/∂m = −qJm + kBT tanh−1m − h = 0   ⟹   m = tanh β(qJm + h)

6. Why the two curves touch

At a solution, β(qJm + h) = tanh−1m. Two identities follow:

ln[2 cosh(tanh−1m)] = ln 2 − ½ ln(1 − m²)
p+ ln p+ + p− ln p− = −ln 2 + ½ ln(1 − m²) + m tanh−1m

Substitute the second into a − hm and use kBT m tanh−1m = qJm² + hm:

a − hm = qJm²/2 − kBT ln 2 + ½kBT ln(1 − m²) = g

So the curves agree in value at every solution, as the Legendre relation a = g + hm requires, even though their shapes differ elsewhere.

7. Stability and the spinodal

A solution is a minimum (stable or metastable) when the curvature is positive:

∂²(a − hm)/∂m² = −qJ + kBT/(1 − m²) > 0   ⟺   m² > 1 − T/Tc

Below Tc, a metastable valley vanishes when its minimum merges with the hump, at the spinodal ms² = 1 − T/Tc. Putting this into Eq. 3 solved for h gives the field at which the ball jumps in the hysteresis graph:

hs = kBT tanh−1ms − qJms

The curvature is also the inverse susceptibility, χ = ∂m/∂h = [−qJ + kBT/(1 − m²)]−1, which is the readout in the left column.

8. Expansion near Tc and the exponents

At h = 0, expand Eq. 2 using ln cosh x ≈ x²/2 − x4/12:

g ≈ −kBT ln 2 + (qJ/2)(1 − Tc/T) m² + (kBT/12)(Tc/T)4 m4     (Eq. 4)

The quartic coefficient is always positive. The quadratic coefficient changes sign at Tc, which is why one valley splits into two. Minimizing gives m0² ≈ 3(−t), so β = 1/2 (see the m vs. T graph). Solving Eq. 3 for h with tanh−1m ≈ m + m³/3 gives

h ≈ kB(T − Tc)m + (kBT/3)m³

At T = Tc, m ∼ h1/3, so δ = 3. Above Tc, χ = 1/[kB(T − Tc)], so γ = 1. The heat capacity jumps by 3kB/2 per spin at Tc, so α = 0.

Things to try

  • At h = 0, lower T through Tc and watch one valley split into two.
  • At T = Tc, the bottom of the well is flat (quartic only) and χ becomes infinite.
  • At T = 0.7Tc, click Sweep h. The ball stays in the higher valley until it disappears at ±hs, tracing a hysteresis loop.
